I like to read ebooks in English. English is my second language, and from time to time I encounter an unknown word when reading. When I do, it takes me out of my reading flow, as I have to look the word up, and try to integrate the meaning of it into what I was reading. It doesn't happen often, and it's not essential for getting the plot, but when it happens it's annoying, because I want to enjoy the text deeply without getting distracted.

I'm thinking of a tool that would ingest an ebook I'm about to read, extract the vocabulary that's unique to that book, and then allow be to learn that vocabulary allowing me to quiz myself until I get it right.

I should be able to view the list of books I've imported into the tool, and for each book I want to be able to view the unique vocabulary for that book, and to quiz myself on it.

I'm not sure how to extract the unique vocabulary from a book. Maybe it could use a word frequency list (how common each word is in English), maybe it could use something like CEFR levels. Maybe it could ingest multiple books in my library, and use that to compute vocabulary deltas (common vocabulary could be words that occur in every book that we could ignore, and unique vocabulary could be words that are only in this specific book, or maybe words in the book that are not in the "common" vocabulary"). Maybe something else; suggest other approaches.

Viewing the book vocabulary: It should give me a list of words with their definitions (where to get those from?), translations to my native language (Lithuanian), and an option to view the word in its context in the book (to view an excerpt with the word highlighted). I'm not sure how to limit the "unique" vocabulary to a reasonable amount of words. Maybe we assign each word multiple numbers (like uniqueness in English, uniqueness in the library, occurrences in the book), compute a compound score, and then list the words ordered by the score, and allow browsing the paginated list, ordered from the most obscure to the most common?

Quizzing: There are several aspects to this. When I choose to quiz myself, the app should select 20 (quiz size; we may make it configurable later) words from the book's vocabulary to quiz me on. The words are selected using a score that is first generated, and then updated as I quiz myself, of how likely am I to not know the word (i.e. need to learn it). The quiz word list is shuffled, and then words are given me one at a time, and I have to provide a correct translation or definition. As I press Enter (or a button), the app then should show the actual translation and definition, with a visual indication if my answer is correct, and a button "Next", if there are words left in the quiz, or "Finish" if there are none. When answering, I may have pressed Enter without actually entering an answer; it means I didn't know the answer. If my answer is correct, the word is removed from the word list of the current quiz. If it is not correct, the word is moved to the end of the word list of the current quiz, and I will have to encounter it again until I answer it correctly. While I'm in the quiz, the quiz UI should show my progress through the quiz - number of correct answers vs the quiz size. It can be presented as a ratio or as a progress bar.

Ingesting: All the books are going to be stored in a directory on disk. We should have an ingestion tool that can be a TUI console application that would accept filename pattern(s) and import those into the tool's database. If the book is already imported (the tool should check the book against the imported books in the database: the filesize, and if that matches, the SHA hash for the book; the database should store filenames, but not use them for checking if the book was already imported, as the filenames are prone to changing), the tool should skip the book, unless we provide a `--force` parameter (which may be useful if we change something about ingestion or indexing).

Tech stack: I'm suggesting Go for the backend, Typescript for the frontend, Sqlite for storage.
